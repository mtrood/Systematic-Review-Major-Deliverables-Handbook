# Stage 1: Defining Your Review

---

## 1.1 Define Your Research Question

- Things to consider when undertaking research:
  - State of the literature on the topic
  - Gaps in the literature
    - Particularly where a lot of literature exists but hasn't been systematically reviewed, or hasn't been systematically reviewed in a long time
  - Is a systematic review appropriate to answer the question?
    - SRs are well-suited to answering large, yet specific, population-based questions
      - **Examples:**
        - Is there an association between early maternal depression and infanticide?
        - Do post-incarceration risk of reoffending instruments accurately predict short-term recidivism among men?
        - Do CBT interventions with individuals convicted of sex offences reduce subsequent sex offences?
  - See Lasserson et al. (2019) for more detailed guidance on starting a review.

---

## 1.2 Identify Your PICO / PICOT / SPIDER Framework

- The next step is to define your PICO, PICOT, or SPIDER — this should follow on naturally from your research question.

- Generally, aim to be **as specific as possible** for each element of the PICO (some exceptions apply):
  - Broad PICO criteria can expand the scope of the review considerably. For example, consider how the search, extraction, and analysis will differ between: a systematic review of the effect of *mental illness* on infanticide, compared with a systematic review of *depressive disorders* on infanticide.

- Some aspects may be arbitrary (e.g., how long after the intervention you will measure the outcome).
  - > <span style="background-color: #fff3cd; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">💡 **Pro-tip:** Note these arbitrary aspects down for later — they will be worth mentioning in your write-up, and you may even be able to control for them in a sensitivity analysis in your results.</span>

---

- **P — Population**
  - Things to bear in mind: if you have multiple populations, are the populations comparable in a synthesis?
  - Examples:
    - Mothers who have a child under 10 years old
    - Males who have spent 12+ months in gaol
    - Male and female sex offenders

- **I — Intervention**
  - Things to bear in mind: how uniform is the intervention?
  - Examples:
    - Depression during the first 10 years post-birth of child
    - Tool for assessing risk of reoffending post-incarceration release
    - CBT interventions for sex offenders 
  - Note: intervention can be replaced with an exposure.
  - Example:
    - Exposure to child maltreament.

- **C — Comparison**
  - Who are the comparison participants?
  - > <span style="background-color: #fff3cd; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">💡 **Pro-tip:** The ideal comparison groups are those that are *randomised* into the control group. Short of this, consider how (and whether) comparison participants are matched to treatment participants. The methods used for matching treatment and comparison participants greatly affects the comparability of the groups.</span>

- **O — Outcome**
  - Bear in mind: whether the outcomes you want to include take the same form (e.g., are they all continuous, count, or categorical data, and do they all intend to measure the same phenomenon?)
  - Examples:
    - Infanticide (yes/no)
    - Re-offences, parole violations
    - Continuous self-reported measures

- **T — Time**
  - Not always required in the PICO but should at least be given consideration, especially in relation to the outcome
  - Examples:
    - 10 years from the birth of a child
    - Reoffending within 5 years of release from prison
    - Any sex offence post-intervention

- An alternative approach that is particularly useful for reviews of qualitative research is **SPIDER**

## 1.3 Check for Existing or Registered Reviews

> <span style="background-color: #ffe0e0; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">⚠️ **Critical step:** You must be certain that (a) no one else has *already completed* the same review, or (b) someone is not *currently* conducting the same review. Some exceptions apply: a large period of time may have passed since a similar review, and you may be undertaking a review to identify and include research conducted (published or otherwise) since the original review.</span>

- Places to look:
  - Google Scholar using your keywords and `"systematic review"`:
    - e.g., `"maternal infanticide" AND "depression" AND "systematic review"`
  - Systematic review registries:
    - [PROSPERO](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/)
    - [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/)
    - [Epistemonikos](https://www.epistemonikos.org/)
