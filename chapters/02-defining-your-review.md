# Stage 1: Defining Your Review

---

## 1.1 Define Your Research Question

Things to consider when formulating your research question:

- **State of the literature** on the topic
- **Gaps in the literature** — particularly where a lot of literature exists but hasn't been systematically reviewed, or hasn't been reviewed in a long time
- **Is a systematic review appropriate** to answer the question?
  - SRs are well-suited to answering large yet specific, population-based questions

**Examples of well-formed research questions:**

- Is there an association between early maternal depression and infanticide?
- Do post-incarceration risk of reoffending tools predict recidivism among men?
- Do CBT interventions with sex offenders reduce subsequent sex offences?

> See Lasserson et al. (2019) for more detailed guidance on starting a review.

---

## 1.2 Identify Your PICO / PICOT / SPIDER Framework

The next step is to define your PICO, PICOT, or SPIDER — this should follow on naturally from your research question.

Generally, aim to be **as specific as possible** for each element of the PICO (some exceptions apply). Broad PICO criteria can expand the scope of a review considerably. For example, consider how the search, extraction, and analysis will differ between:

- A systematic review of the effect of *mental illness* on infanticide, versus
- A systematic review of *depressive disorders* on infanticide

Some aspects may be arbitrary (e.g., how long after an intervention you will measure the outcome).

> <span style="background-color: #fff3cd; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">💡 **Pro-tip:** Note these arbitrary aspects down for later — they are worth mentioning in your write-up, and you may be able to control for them in a sensitivity analysis.</span>

---

### P — Population

<u>Things to bear in mind:</u> if you have multiple populations, are they comparable in a synthesis?

**Examples:**

- Mothers who have a child under 10 years old
- Males who have spent 12+ months in gaol
- Male and female sex offenders

---

### I — Intervention

<u>Things to bear in mind:</u> how uniform is the intervention?

**Examples:**

- Depression during the first 10 years post-birth of child
- A tool for assessing risk of reoffending post-incarceration release
- CBT interventions for sex offenders

---

### C — Comparison

Who are the comparison participants?

> <span style="background-color: #fff3cd; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">💡 **Pro-tip:** The best comparison groups are those *randomised* into the control group. Short of this, consider how (and whether) comparison participants are matched to treatment participants — the matching method greatly affects any assessment of causality.</span>

**Examples:**

- Women without children
- Individuals who score below the tool's threshold, or individuals who do not receive the assessment but spent 12+ months in gaol
- Male and female sex offenders who did not receive the CBT intervention:
  - Those who received another intervention
  - Those who received no other intervention

---

### O — Outcome

<u>Things to bear in mind:</u> whether the outcomes you intend to include take the same form (e.g., are they all continuous, count, or categorical data, and do they all measure the same phenomenon?).

**Examples:**

- Infanticide (yes/no)
- Re-offences, parole violations
- Continuous self-reported measures

---

### T — Time

Not always required in the PICO but should at least be given consideration, particularly in relation to the outcome.

**Examples:**

- 10 years from the birth of a child
- Reoffending within 5 years of release from prison
- Any sex offence post-intervention

---

### SPIDER — An Alternative Framework

An alternative approach that is particularly useful for reviews of **qualitative research** is the SPIDER framework.

## 1.3 Check for Existing or Registered Reviews

> <span style="background-color: #ffe0e0; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 3px;">⚠️ **Critical step:** You must be certain that (a) no one else has *already completed* the same review, or (b) someone is not *currently* conducting the same review.</span>

Some exceptions apply — a large period of time may have passed since a similar review, and you may be undertaking a review specifically to identify and include research published since the original.

**Places to look:**

- **Google Scholar** — search your keywords with `"systematic review"`:
  - e.g., `"maternal infanticide" AND "depression" AND "systematic review"`
- **Systematic review registries:**
  - [PROSPERO](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/)
  - [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/)
  - [Epistemonikos](https://www.epistemonikos.org/)
